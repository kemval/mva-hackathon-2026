01

The correction that reorganises everything

Suijkerbuijk 2010

MVA is caused by loss of a mitotic checkpoint protein, so the natural move is to look for a drug that repairs or bypasses the checkpoint. That move is wrong, and it is wrong in a specific, published way.

× The intuitive reading

“A checkpoint kinase is broken. Find something that restores checkpoint signalling, or that kills the aneuploid cells it lets through.”

Leads directly to SAC-targeted agents and to aneuploidy-selective cytotoxics. Both are dead ends here — and one is actively harmful (§05).

✓ What patient cells actually show

Cell lines from MVA patients with this same architecture — a truncating allele paired with a kinase-domain missense allele — have 2–6× less BubR1 protein. The truncated allele makes none; the missense allele makes protein that is degraded too fast.

The paper’s own conclusion: chromosomal instability in these patients “is a result of low BUBR1 protein abundance.”

The residual protein is competent, just insufficient — it still localises correctly to kinetochores. This is a dosage problem in a working machine, not a broken machine.

02

Two results carry the whole argument

Why abundance is the target

Restoring abundance restores function

Forced overexpression of two kinase-domain missense mutants, back to wild-type levels, fully restored the mitotic checkpoint response.

One of those two, L1012P, sits ten residues from this child’s p.Asn1002Lys.

“These mutations do not impose significant constraints on BUBR1 function other than affecting overall BUBR1 protein abundance.”

And there is a threshold

Titrating BubR1 down with an inducible shRNA:

~6% of normal bad
~13% of normal fine

At 6%, most cells missegregate chromosomes. At 13%, segregation is “virtually” unaffected. A few percentage points of protein separate failing from functioning.

Stated as the inference it is. p.Asn1002Lys itself was never tested. The prediction rests on domain membership — and Q921H, inside the same domain, behaves exactly like wild-type. Structural modelling on AlphaFold AF-O60566-F1 makes that worse, not better: Q921 is more buried than Asn1002 (relative solvent accessibility 1.1% vs 16.2%) and also makes buried side-chain hydrogen bonds — so every feature that would mark residue 1002 as critical is present at a position where substitution demonstrably did nothing to protein level. Position does not guarantee instability, and in this protein that failure is documented. This is why §07 leads with an assay rather than a prescription.

Five genes return zero in both databases: BUB1B, BUB3, CDC20, MAD2L1, NMNAT1.

The abundance tier’s 30 is misleading: 23 of them sit in the two genes this analysis excludes (HSP90AA1 and PARP1). Subtract those and four usable drugs remain — all anti-CD38 antibodies — plus three NAMPT inhibitors that push NAD⁺ the wrong way.

And there is no SIRT2 activator anywhere in the searched space. Every SIRT2 compound in either database is an inhibitor or an unranked low-confidence claim. That is exactly why the abundance mechanism has to be reached through substrate supply — an NAD⁺ precursor — rather than through a drug against the enzyme.

04

The pharmacopeia is built of inhibitors. This disease is a deficiency.

The structural mismatch
129of 142 action-typed Open Targets entries are INHIBITOR

Counting antisense inhibitors, RNAi inhibitors and antagonists brings it to 95% inhibitory. Across all 24 genes there is exactly one ACTIVATOR and one STABILISER.

DGIdb agrees independently: among approved-drug interactions across these genes, 62 are inhibitory and 1 is activating — and that one is granisetron against TP53, an antiemetic. It is a spurious annotation of exactly the kind DGIdb’s own disclaimer warns about.

This generalises well past this child. Drug development produces inhibitors, because binding and blocking a protein is chemically tractable and most common diseases involve too much of something. Loss-of-function rare disease is the mirror image — and most rare disease is loss of function. The practical corollary: ask “what raises this protein?” rather than “what binds it?”, and expect the best answer to be a metabolite or a substrate, because those are among the few things that push a system up.

05

The inversion: the top search hit is the most contraindicated agent

Gate G1

What a keyword search surfaces first

Search “drugs for aneuploidy” and the best-known paper is Tang et al. 2011, which identifies 17-AAG among three aneuploidy-selective compounds.

Why it would harm this child

17-AAG is an HSP90 inhibitor. The mutant BubR1 in MVA patients is an HSP90 client: geldanamycin severely reduced mutant but not wild-type BubR1, and with cycloheximide it “removed virtually all mutant BUBR1 protein.”

Blocking HSP90 here is predicted to destroy the residual protein the child depends on — driving the primary defect in the wrong direction.

It fails a second gate as well: its mechanism is selective killing of aneuploid cells. That concept was built for tumours, where aneuploid cells are the disease. In mosaic variegated aneuploidy those cells are the patient, distributed across tissues by definition of the syndrome.

The exclusion gates were written and committed before the search ran, which is the only reason this was caught rather than rationalised afterwards. Pathway proximity is not a therapeutic rationale.

Highest overall — and it is not a medicine

Nicotinamide riboside is a dietary supplement. It leads because it is the only candidate acting on the defect rather than its consequences, and it carries a randomised placebo-controlled trial in a monogenic progeroid disease plus paediatric exposure down to age 1.

Highest approved medicine

Dasatinib + quercetin. Dasatinib is licensed from age 1 — but none of 22 trials of the senolytic regimen ever enrolled a child, and TKIs are documented to impair growth in an already growth-restricted child.

Scores are structured arguments, not measurements. Two candidates one point apart are not meaningfully distinguishable. Metformin reaches 10 almost entirely on safety and familiarity (C 4, E 3) with the weakest mechanism in the top half — it is the safest option here, not the most targeted.

07

The first recommendation is not a drug

Measure before treating

A western blot for BubR1 in patient PBMCs or fibroblasts, against matched controls. It is cheap, standard, and it forks the entire report.

If reduced but present

The hypothesis is live

This is the hypomorphic state the models represent. The 6%–13% threshold means a modest gain in abundance could cross a real functional line, and the abundance tier is worth pursuing.

If absent

The top tier is futile

There is nothing to stabilise. Ranks 1, 2 and 9 fall away entirely and the list should be re-read from the senolytic tier down. Better to learn this from a blot than from a child.

The same assay answers a Track 1 question. A demonstrated abundance defect attributable to p.Asn1002Lys is functional evidence — the substance of ACMG PS3 — for a variant currently classified VUS. One inexpensive experiment tests the therapy and may resolve the classification.

Supporting assays, cheapest first: a cytokinesis-block micronucleus assay (measures the actual missegregation rate rather than a proxy); a karyotype on non-malignant metaphases, which is both Track 1’s first recommended test and the diagnostic the AACR guideline lists for MVA; then senescence markers, a SASP panel, and blood NAD⁺.

Every one of those reassuring calls is an artefact of the assumption, not a measurement. A pipeline that ran the flag and printed the table would hand a clinician a clean pharmacogenomic clearance for thiopurines, irinotecan and fluoropyrimidines that this data cannot support. Irinotecan is used in relapsed rhabdomyosarcoma; UGT1A1 status here is unresolved.

And one false alarm worth catching. PharmCAT reports RYR1 rs35364374 with the CPIC phenotype “Uncertain Susceptibility” to malignant hyperthermia — alarming for a child facing repeated procedures under anaesthesia. Checked against the primary source, ClinGen’s expert panel classifies that exact variant Benign (VCV000093279, 3-star). A pharmacogenomic phenotype label is not a variant classification, and must be checked against one.
