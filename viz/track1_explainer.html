What disrupts the second allele?

A whole-genome search in a child with mosaic variegated aneuploidy returned a confirmed pathogenic nonsense variant in BUB1B and one ultra-rare missense variant 11 kb away. The disease is recessive, so something must disrupt the second copy. This atlas draws every piece of evidence that bears on which candidate does — including the evidence that was tested and thrown out.

Tracks
    01

    From two million records to two candidates

    Pipeline

    Two gene panels, one pipeline, run end to end by scripts in the repository. The primary panel is nine MVA and spindle-checkpoint genes; the secondary is eleven genes chosen for two clinical features that fall outside the MVA spectrum. Every stage below is a command in pipeline/README.md, not a described procedure.

    The detail

    Filter criteria: gnomAD max subpopulation AF < 0.001 and VEP impact MODERATE or HIGH on the canonical transcript. Deep-intronic variants are MODIFIER impact and are therefore discarded by design — that is what the filter is for. One was recovered deliberately, by re-running the same script at --impacts MODIFIER restricted to BUB1B: of 10 rare MODIFIER variants across the nine-gene panel, exactly one sits in BUB1B. That is a narrow stated query, not a hunt through intronic space until something interesting appears.

    The PASS filter removes 7 records from the primary panel and 20 from the secondary. Those were later inspected rather than left unexamined: the only BUB1B record among them is an intronic indel, but eight of the secondary panel's twenty sit in NF1, seven carrying the MQ40 signature of that gene's segmental duplications — a genuine sensitivity gap, disclosed rather than hidden.

    02

    The gene, drawn to scale

    BUB1B · chr15

    All 23 exons at their true coordinates, fetched from Ensembl and checked against three positions the report derived independently. Read it as three questions stacked on one axis: where the variants are, where the genome is provably diploid, and where a deletion could still be hiding.

    Why the hatching matters A hatched band is not a finding. It marks an interval the assay could not interrogate — the largest, 16,809 bp, contains exons 9–16. A multi-exon deletion there remains the leading alternative second allele, and only an assay this pipeline does not have (MLPA, microarray, or read-depth calling on the BAM) can close it.
    The detail

    Across the padded region there are 14 PASS calls at median depth 40 (range 26–48), none below 10, and none showing the depth halving a hemizygous deletion would produce. Eight are heterozygous. The two homozygous-alternate calls inside the exon 9–16 window are formally consistent with hemizygosity, though their entirely normal depth (42 and 36) argues against it.

    Published allelic architecture cuts against the deletion hypothesis. In the four biallelic families of the 2010 Suijkerbuijk series, each pairs a truncating allele with a missense one — truncating alleles yield no transcript, so what patients live on is a residual pool of unstable missense protein. A multi-exon deletion in trans with p.Leu737Ter would be truncating/truncating. Four families is too narrow a base for a universal claim, and the report makes only the narrow one.

    03

    The protein, and the domain that lost its evidence code

    BubR1 · UniProt O60566

    The nonsense variant truncates at residue 737 and removes the entire kinase domain. The missense variant sits inside that domain at residue 1002 — which looks like textbook grounds for the ACMG code PM1. It is not, and the reason is directly below the protein bar.

    The detail

    PVS1 rests on nonsense-mediated decay, checked against real exon structure. c.2210 lies in exon 17 of 23, and the final exon–exon junction falls at CDS position 2957 — placing the premature stop 747 nt upstream of it, far outside the ~50–55 nt window in which a stop escapes NMD. There is no cryptic-splice rescue either: SpliceAI scores this variant 0.03 across all 22 transcripts.

    BubR1 is a pseudokinase (Kops et al. 2012). Suijkerbuijk et al. 2010 showed the kinase domain is dispensable for both mitotic-checkpoint activity and microtubule attachment — their LAP-731X truncation, which lacks the domain outright, restores the nocodazole response and normal segregation — so the "active site of an enzyme" archetype PM1 is written around does not transfer here. PM1 was dropped rather than re-founded on the disease-specific spectrum, because that alternative footing would have traded a wrong argument for an untested one: ClinVar now lists the in-domain members of that same series as Conflicting classifications, while the only BUB1B missense classified Likely pathogenic without conflict lies outside the domain.

    04

    The evidence ladder, and what fell off it

    ACMG / Tavtigian points

    Codes are combined with ClinGen's Bayesian points framework. Variant 1 clears Likely Pathogenic on PVS1 alone. Variant 2 does not clear anything — and the interesting part is the trajectory: it scored 6 points, then 4, then 2, as each supporting code was tested against its own definition and failed.

    The detail

    Which specification applies where. ClinGen SVI is used throughout — PM2 at Supporting, PM3 on the recessive point scheme, and the Tavtigian points framework for combining — rather than SVI where it happens to lower a code and the 2015 defaults where it would not. That consistency is what moved PM3; adopting SVI for PM2 alone would have been exactly the selective reading the report criticises elsewhere.

    One consequence is worth naming: under the strict 2015 combining table, PVS1 plus a single Supporting code would not formally reach Likely Pathogenic (that table wants 1 Very Strong + 1 Moderate). Variant 1's classification therefore rests on the points framework. Either route gives the same answer — but the route is stated rather than glossed.

    05

    Is variant 1 actually rare enough?

    PM2 · interactive

    PM2 asks whether a variant is rarer than the disease could tolerate. The threshold is the Whiffin–Ware maximum credible population allele frequency — and for variant 1 it is a close call that depends entirely on three numbers nobody has published. Move them and watch the code fail.

    A reviewer challenged this formula, and was answered An expert panel proposed moving the allelic term inside the square root. Checked against the framework's own manuscript source, the recessive form is √P × A × √G / √f — A outside, as drawn. The obvious sanity check cannot settle it: at A = 1 both placements return √P. The two forms differ by 1/√A and are distinguishable only at A < 1. Finding declined.
    06

    The code that scored a quarter of a point

    PM3 · ClinGen SVI

    PM3 is the natural code for a second hit in trans with a known pathogenic variant in a recessive gene. Under the 2015 defaults it would be Moderate, worth 2 points. Under the specification this report says it follows, phase is unknown and the other allele is Likely pathogenic — which is worth 0.25.

    07

    Four predictors agree. The calibrated one abstains.

    PP3 · dbNSFP

    It would be easy to report "5 of 6 predictors say damaging" and move on. But ClinGen's SVI group recommends a single calibrated predictor over tool-counting — and against its own published thresholds, that predictor lands in the dead zone.

    08

    A splice screen that found nothing, honestly

    SpliceAI · 2,346 variants

    A splice-altering variant hiding below the impact filter was the most plausible blind spot in this design, so it was closed rather than merely disclosed. Every PASS variant in both panels was scored. Nothing reaches the threshold — and the variants that could not be scored are drawn as what they are.

    09

    The limitation under every negative result

    Callability · 20 genes

    The input is a sites-only VCF. It records where a variant was called and says nothing about where none was — so absence of a call and absence of coverage are indistinguishable. Every negative in this report rests on that undifferentiated absence. As a partial proxy, call density inside each gene is compared with its own flanking sequence.

    10

    The phenotype, including what is missing from it

    PP4 · 8 HPO terms

    Eight coded features against the three classical MVA subtypes. The match to MVA1 is close — but a matrix makes visible what prose let slide: one core MVA1 feature has no code in the supplied list at all, and two coded features sit outside the spectrum entirely.

    The detail

    PP4 is carried qualitatively and scored into neither variant. The MVA1 features present here are individually non-specific; the discriminating one — mosaic aneuploidy on karyotype — has not been assayed; and one core feature is uncoded. Scored formally it would be PP4_Supporting at most, worth 1 point, moving variant 1 from 9 to 10 and so from Likely Pathogenic to Pathogenic — a reclassification this evidence does not warrant.

    Nephrocalcinosis inverted the reasoning. Earlier versions offered two acquired explanations, prematurity and chemotherapy nephrotoxicity, "each sufficient on its own." The phenotype document states in the same table row that the finding is present since birth — and both proposed mechanisms are necessarily postnatal. Congenital onset raises the prior on a monogenic renal or metabolic cause, which makes the 6-gene renal subset run here a materially weaker negative than it was presented as.

    Parental recurrent miscarriage is concordant, not competing. Parents of a child with biallelic BUB1B loss are obligate carriers, so one conception in four is affected. If constitutional aneuploidy on this scale carries high embryonic lethality, recurrent loss is closer to a prediction of the diagnosis than a finding against it. That conditional is doing the work, and it is stated as a conditional deliberately.

    11

    The cheap test nobody has ordered

    Confirmatory assays

    MVA is defined cytogenetically — mosaic aneuploidy across multiple chromosomes in more than a quarter of cells. Not molecularly. An earlier version of this report named that gap and then recommended only the phase test. The order is now corrected: the assay that tests the diagnosis comes before the assay that tests the pairing.

    The asymmetry worth seeing Confirming phase moves EPCR from 0.95 to about 0.98 — a small gain. A negative karyotype would undercut the diagnosis itself, in a way no amount of further variant-level evidence repairs. The residual 5% is dominated by a test that costs almost nothing and has never been run.
