# Surveillance after a first tumour in MVA — read against the primary recommendation

Captured 2026-09-02 for **Task 6** of `pipeline/gap_closure_brief.md`.
**Revised the same day after obtaining the primary source**, which corrects an earlier
version of this file. See "Correction of record" at the end.

**Primary source obtained: Hol JA, Jewell R, Chowdhury T, Duncan C, Nakata K, Oue T, et al.,
"Wilms tumour surveillance in at-risk children: Literature review and recommendations from
the SIOP-Europe Host Genome Working Group and SIOP Renal Tumour Study Group," *European

## 1. What the primary says about MVA that Nakano's summary does not

> "MVA can be caused by biallelic *BUB1B*, *TRIP13* or *CEP57* pathogenic variants, while in
> some patients, the cause remains unknown. **WTs have been reported in approximately 50% of
> patients with *BUB1B* variants** [refs 101,102], 20% of patients with *TRIP13* variants and,
> to our current knowledge, none of the reported patients with *CEP57* variants or MVA because
> of an unknown cause. Because of the limited number of reported patients, **we recommend WT
> surveillance for all patients with cytogenetically confirmed MVA.**"

**The ~50% figure is new to this project.** Nakano 2024 states the recommendation without
quantifying the risk it is built on. Two observations follow.

First, it is a **strong** number — this is the highest Wilms risk in the guideline's "other
syndromes" section, above Fanconi D1 (~20%), Fanconi N (~40%) and Mulibrey nanism (6–8%). It
strengthens the report's surveillance argument rather than weakening it.

Second, its sources are **Jacquemont 2002** and **Hanks 2004** — small early case series. The
guideline's own framing is "because of the **limited number of reported patients**", and the
denominator behind "approximately 50%" is not stated in the text. Ascertainment bias runs one
way in a literature where patients are published because they had tumours. Treat 50% as the
guideline's considered estimate, not as a measured incidence.

Note also **"cytogenetically confirmed"** — the recommendation is anchored to karyotype, which
independently corroborates Track 1's decision to lead with karyotype as the confirmatory test.

## 2. The age-7 cutoff has a stated rationale — and for MVA it is *borrowed*

This is the finding an earlier version of this file got wrong by not reading the primary.

> "If WT surveillance is indicated, we recommend continuing surveillance until a child's **7th
> birthday regardless of the underlying CPS diagnosis**. By the age of 7, 90% of sporadic WTs,
> 94% of WTs in children with BWS and >95% of WTs in children with *WT1*-related syndromes
> have been diagnosed, and this age has been previously recommended by other groups. **For
> other CPS, the number of reported patients with WT was too small to determine this
> percentage.**"

So the cutoff **is** justified — but the evidence generating it comes from sporadic Wilms, BWS
and *WT1* syndromes. **MVA falls in the "other CPS" category where the guideline says the
numbers are too small to compute the figure.** The age-7 endpoint applied to this child is a
harmonised convention, not an MVA-derived one, and the guideline is transparent about that.

## 3. Prior tumour *is* addressed — for a different tumour, and in the direction of shortening

> "Although we have previously suggested screening patients with *WT1*-related syndromes until
> the age of 5 years and **to prolong surveillance only for patients with a prior diagnosis of
> WT/nephroblastomatosis**, the consensus group agreed to recommend surveillance until the 7th
> birthday for all CPS including *WT1*-related syndromes. Factors that influenced this decision
> were that nephroblastomatosis may not be identified on ultrasound, to maintain consistency
> with other WT predisposition genes/syndromes and **in response to patient/parent
> representatives' views.**"

The guideline therefore does consider a prior tumour — but only a prior **renal** tumour, and
only as a historical reason to *extend* surveillance that has since been superseded by
harmonising everyone to 7. **A prior rhabdomyosarcoma is nowhere in this reasoning.**

## 4. The silence on non-renal surveillance is explicit and by design

> "For these syndromes, the estimated WT risk was considered too low to recommend targeted WT
> surveillance, although **cancer surveillance for other tumour types is warranted in some of
> these conditions (but outside the scope of this guideline).**"

**This is the cleanest answer to Task 6's question.** The guideline does not omit
rhabdomyosarcoma surveillance by oversight — it **scopes non-Wilms surveillance out and says
so**. Nakano 2024's MVA section then adds only "regular clinical assessment including review of
systems to identify signs of rhabdomyosarcoma and other malignancies," with no modality, no
interval, and no endpoint.

So for a child with MVA who has already had a rhabdomyosarcoma, the position across both
sources is: **a well-specified renal protocol, and for everything else, clinical review with no
protocol attached.** That gap is real, documented, and acknowledged by the guideline itself.

## 5. Why ultrasound — the answer is not "because radiation must be avoided"

> "Although CT or MRI may have a higher resolution for discriminating between different tumour
> types and nephrogenic rests, **ultrasonography is believed to be equally effective for initial
> WT detection based on expert consensus.**"

Ultrasound is not a second-best modality chosen to spare radiation; it is judged **equally
effective** for the detection task. The radiation-avoidance point and the modality choice are
independent, and the report should not present the protocol as a compromise forced by
radiosensitivity.

**Interval rationale, also stated:** "WTs are known to have a high growth rate with the
shortest reported estimated doubling time being **11 days**… the consensus group agreed that an
interval of ≥4 months risks higher tumour stage at diagnosis."

**Start:** "We recommend initiating surveillance **at birth or as soon as a CPS is diagnosed**…
surveillance can be initiated based on the clinical suspicion of a CPS, while awaiting test
results."

**Technique (Table 1), which no summary of this guideline carries:** high-frequency probes and
paediatric settings — linear >10 MHz in infants, curvilinear >6 MHz and linear >10 MHz in
toddlers and children; no fasting or bladder preparation; kidney only for MVA (full abdominal
reserved for BWS/BWSp, lateralised overgrowth, SGBS); whole renal parenchyma imaged in
longitudinal and transverse planes, **both supine and prone**; performed by someone experienced
in paediatric ultrasonography, with screen-detected lesions managed at a specialist centre.

## 6. Radiation sensitivity in MVA — still no syndrome-specific evidence

Unchanged by the primary source, which does not discuss radiosensitivity. A targeted PubMed
search — `(mosaic variegated aneuploidy OR BUB1B OR premature chromatid separation) AND
(radiosensitiv* OR "radiation sensitiv*" OR irradiat*)` — returned **five records, all
wrong-product**: radiation-induced chromatid separation in carcinoma cells, BUB1B in
breast-cancer homologous recombination, a mouse *Apc* QTL study, phototherapy on fibroblasts,
visceral obesity. None concerns MVA patients.

Nakano's "Radiation exposure should be avoided" rests on a **class** statement — "**many** of
these genomic instability disorders also present with exquisite sensitivity to anticancer
treatments" — and where that paper documents hypersensitivity it names the syndrome
(ataxia-telangiectasia, Nijmegen breakage syndrome). **MVA is not among them.** The advice is
sound as precaution, and on the general position that "no amount of radiation should be
considered absolutely safe"; it is not an MVA finding. Matrix **C16**.

## 7. A precedent from an adjacent syndrome — cited as precedent only

Li-Fraumeni is where the field has worked out surveillance for a patient who must avoid
radiation. **Frebourg T et al., *Eur J Hum Genet* 2020, PMID 32457520:** radiotherapy and
genotoxic chemotherapy "contribute to the development of subsequent primary tumours," testing
should precede treatment so these can be avoided in carriers, and paediatric surveillance is
6-monthly clinical examination and abdominal ultrasound plus annual whole-body and brain MRI —
**every modality non-ionising**. **Kratz CP et al., *Clin Cancer Res* 2017 (PMID 28572266)**
records AACR adopting a modified "Toronto protocol".

**This is a precedent for the shape of a solution, not evidence about MVA.** LFS is a dominant
single-gene syndrome with a large evidence base; MVA has under 50 described patients. Nothing
here may be represented as an MVA recommendation.

## Correction of record

An earlier version of this file, written before the primary was obtained, stated that Hol 2021
was **"closed access, no open deposit"** and **"unobtainable"**, and that the guideline gave
**"no rationale for stopping renal ultrasound at 7."** All three were wrong.

The paper is **CC-BY open access**. The error came from reading Europe PMC's `isOpenAccess: N`
as "not open access" when it means "not open access **in Europe PMC**" — a statement about
deposit location, not licence. OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar both report `is_oa: true`,
`oa_status: hybrid`, licence CC-BY. And the age-7 rationale is stated plainly in section 6 of
the paper; the finding is not that a rationale is absent but that **for MVA it is borrowed**
from syndromes with larger case counts.

What survives from the earlier version: the guideline **is** silent on post-first-tumour
surveillance and on rhabdomyosarcoma imaging — now on the stronger footing that it scopes
non-Wilms surveillance out explicitly — and there **is** no MVA-specific radiosensitivity
evidence.

## LIMITS — what this file does NOT show

- **It does not establish that surveillance should change after a first tumour.** It establishes
  that neither source addresses the question, and that the guideline excludes non-Wilms
  surveillance from its scope by design.
- **The ~50% WT risk is the guideline's estimate from small early series** (Jacquemont 2002,
  Hanks 2004), with no denominator given and ascertainment bias running toward overestimation.
- **COG LTFU v6.0 was not obtained.** `survivorshipguidelines.org` did not resolve from this
  environment (connection failure, not a paywall). Whether its new genetic-predisposition
  sections name MVA is still unknown.
- **Supplemental Table 5 and Table 2 were not read**, only the main text and Table 1.
- **The radiosensitivity negative is one English-language PubMed search.** Task 3's non-English
  literature is untouched and could hold Japanese PCS-era work, since chromosomal breakage
  studies were that field's core method.
- Nothing here is a treatment recommendation, and no source addresses this child.

## Evidence tier

**Expert-consensus guideline**, now read in the primary rather than in summary — the strongest
tier available in this project, and still consensus rather than trial evidence, in a syndrome
the guideline itself describes as having too few reported patients to compute its key figure.
The LFS material is **guideline evidence about a different syndrome**. The radiation-avoidance
advice is **precautionary class generalisation**.
