# Mosaic-variegated aneuploidy syndrome mutation or haploinsufficiency in Cep57 impairs tumor suppression

Authors: Aziz K, Sieben CJ, Jeganathan KB, Hamada M, Davies BA, Velasco ROF, Rahman N, Katzmann DJ, van Deursen JM (Mayo Clinic and Institute of Cancer Research)
Journal: Journal of Clinical Investigation, Volume 128, Issue 8, July 23 2018. DOI: 10.1172/JCI120316
Source: https://www.jci.org/articles/view/120316 (open access)
Captured: 2026-08-31

## Summary

Mouse-model study of CEP57 mutation/haploinsufficiency. Cep57^T/T mice died within 24 hours after birth (short curly tails, severely impaired vertebral ossification). Key findings:
- Osteoblasts showed Fgf2 deficiency
- Broad tissue aneuploidy at birth
- Failed centrosome maturation during G2 phase
- Heterozygous mice showed cancer predisposition WITHOUT loss of heterozygosity

## Bone development defects
Defective vertebral ossification linked to reduced Fgf2 expression specifically in bone tissue — "Cep57 exerts this role through its interaction with Fgf2."

## Centrosome abnormalities
- 84% of Cep57^T/T metaphase cells had supernumerary centrioles (vs. 4% wild-type)
- Premature centriole disengagement during metaphase
- Impaired PCM (pericentriolar material) component recruitment
- Reduced spindle microtubule nucleation

## Chromosome segregation & in vivo aneuploidy
Markedly increased chromosome missegregation rates; significantly increased aneuploid cells across lung, liver, kidney, skin, brain, and vertebrae tissues.

## Discussion / conclusions
Cep57 has three distinct biological roles: (1) bone formation via Fgf2 regulation, (2) centrosome maturation ("master regulator"), (3) tumor suppression as a haploinsufficient tumor suppressor.

**Cancer findings**: heterozygous mice had elevated tumor incidence (65% at 16 months vs. 24% controls), primarily lung adenomas. Tumors retained Cep57 expression despite only one functional copy (haploinsufficiency mechanism, not classic two-hit).

**Key mechanistic distinction**: unlike BUB1B and TRIP13 MVA mutations, CEP57 deficiency did NOT impair mitotic checkpoint function — suggesting a DIFFERENT mechanistic route to chromosomal instability and cancer risk (centrosome-amplification-driven CIN, not spindle-checkpoint-driven CIN). Authors suggest intensified cancer screening may be warranted for CEP57 mutation carriers.
