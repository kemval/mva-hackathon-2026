## Foundational / gene-discovery papers (fetch these first)

*Author attribution corrected 2026-08-31 after direct PubMed verification — the MVA Society's listing page credited all three to "Rahman N," but Rahman is the senior/corresponding author on each, not first author. Cite by first author per standard convention.*

- **Hanks S et al., "Constitutional aneuploidy and cancer predisposition caused by biallelic mutations in BUB1B."** Nature Genetics 36:1159-1161, Nov 2004. PMID 15475955. Rahman N is senior author. **Abstract directly verified via PubMed eutils**: "Mosaic variegated aneuploidy is a rare recessive condition characterized by growth retardation, microcephaly, childhood cancer and constitutional mosaicism for chromosomal gains and losses. In five families with mosaic variegated aneuploidy, including two with embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, we identified truncating and missense mutations of BUB1B... These data are the first to relate germline mutations in a spindle checkpoint gene with a human disorder and strongly support a causal link between aneuploidy and cancer development." **THE original BUB1B-MVA paper.**
- **Snape K et al., "Mutations in CEP57 cause mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndrome."** Nature Genetics 43(6):527-9, June 2011. PMID 21552266, PMC3508359 (open access — not yet fetched in full). Rahman N is senior author. **Abstract directly verified via PubMed eutils**: "Using exome sequencing and a variant prioritization strategy that focuses on loss-of-function variants, we identified biallelic, loss-of-function CEP57 mutations as a cause of constitutional mosaic aneuploidies. CEP57 is a centrosomal protein and is involved in nucleating and stabilizing microtubules." The original CEP57-MVA paper.
- **Yost S et al., "Biallelic TRIP13 mutations predispose to Wilms tumor and chromosome missegregation."** Nature Genetics 49(7):1148-1151, July 2017. PMID 28553959. Rahman N and Kops GJPL are among the senior/co-senior authors. **Abstract directly verified via PubMed eutils** (title/authors confirmed; full abstract text retrieval was truncated in this pass — low priority to re-fetch given the title and authorship are already confirmed). The original TRIP13-MVA paper.
- "Biallelic germline mutations in MAD1L1 induce a syndrome of aneuploidy with high tumor susceptibility" — Science, Miguel Urioste, Nov 2022. A newer/rarer related gene not on the classic BUB1B/CEP57/TRIP13 list — worth checking during unbiased variant filtering.

## Mechanism / aging-biology papers (Track 2 flagship candidates)
- **"SIRT2 induces the checkpoint kinase BubR1 to increase lifespan"** — EMBO Journal, David A. Sinclair, May 2014 (PMID 24825348, open access PMC4194088). **Abstract confirmed via search**: BubR1 hypomorphic mice age faster; BubR1 levels decline with age in wild-type mice due to declining NAD+ and reduced SIRT2-mediated deacetylation of BubR1 (lysine-668), counteracted by acetyltransferase CBP. **Overexpressing SIRT2 OR treating mice with the NAD+ precursor nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) increases BubR1 abundance in vivo; SIRT2 overexpression in BubR1-hypomorphic mice increases median lifespan (greater effect in males).** — **This is a second, independent, and arguably even stronger mechanistic lead than the Baker et al. 2011 senolytic angle: it directly links an NAD+-boosting intervention (NMN, an over-the-counter/investigational supplement with a strong existing human safety/trial record) to increased BubR1 protein levels specifically** — much closer to "supplementing the exact protein this disease is short of" than senolytics are.
- **"Promising Results With NAD Supplementation in Rare Diseases With Premature Aging and DNA Damage"** — Aging Cell, Vilhelm A. Bohr, Dec 2025 (full text fetched: `14_bohr2026_nad_supplementation.md`). General review of NAD+ supplementation across premature-aging/DNA-repair-disorder rare diseases — not MVA-specific, but directly supports the NAD+/NMN repurposing angle's real-world clinical plausibility and safety profile discussion.
- "Reduced life and healthspan in mice carrying a mono-allelic BubR1 MVA mutation" — Jan M. van Deursen, Dec 2012. Mouse model directly relevant to hypomorphic (not null) BUB1B genotypes — likely the most relevant animal model if the case turns out to be a compound-het with at least one hypomorphic allele.
- "The Vertebrate Mitotic Checkpoint Protein BUBR1 Is an Unusual Pseudokinase" — Geert J.P.L. Kops, ScienceDirect, June 2012. Structural/domain-biology paper — useful for ACMG PM1 (functional domain) argument once the exact variant position is known.
- "High-throughput tracking enables systematic phenotyping and drug repurposing in C. elegans disease models" — eLife, André Brown, Jan 2025. Methodology paper — potentially useful as a citable precedent for the Scalability criterion (a systematic, model-organism-based repurposing screening approach), not for MVA-specific findings.

## Use in this project
Work through the "Mechanism / aging-biology papers" section first during Weeks 3-4 — the SIRT2/NMN lead (Sinclair 2014) should be evaluated alongside the Baker 2011 senolytic lead as a co-flagship or alternative Track 2 hypothesis; NMN/NAD+ precursor supplementation may actually be the stronger candidate given it (a) has a more direct mechanistic link (restoring BubR1 protein levels specifically, not just clearing downstream senescent cells) and (b) NMN has a substantial existing human safety/trial track record as a supplement. Compare both honestly in the Track 2 report rather than anchoring on whichever was found first.
