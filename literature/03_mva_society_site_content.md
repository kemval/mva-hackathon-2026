## Organization overview
"The only organisation in the world dedicated to Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy (MVA)" — ultra-rare condition affecting fewer than 50 people globally. Mission: fund research, build community support, develop treatments. Approximately 40 known cases globally as of capture. First MVA Research Conference held/scheduled at Great Ormond Street Hospital, London. Medical Advisory Board includes Professor Anil Dhawan (paediatric liver medicine/rare disease), Professor Harry Leitch (clinical geneticist, GOSH), Paediatric Consultant Sharon Jheeta, Dr Ellie Seaby (paediatrics/genetics).

## "MVA Explained" page content
MVA affects how cells divide/grow — produces high numbers of cells with the wrong chromosome count (aneuploidy). "Mosaic" = mix of normal/abnormal cells; "variegated" = diverse types of chromosomal errors — hence symptoms vary person to person.

Effects: delays in physical growth/development, distinct facial features/body differences, low muscle tone, learning disabilities, significantly increased cancer risk.

Notably, the Society publishes a dedicated downloadable guide: **"MVA Syndrome - BUB1B gene variants explained"** — explicitly framed for families/healthcare professionals managing MVA1/BUB1B-caused disease specifically. (This does not confirm this hackathon case's exact gene — the Society covers MVA generally — but it signals BUB1B is the subtype the Society has prioritized enough to write a dedicated public guide for, consistent with it being the most common/best-characterized subtype.)

"MVA is so rare that most doctors will never see a case in their entire career... Very little research has been done. Families often receive little or no guidance at diagnosis. There are no established treatment pathways or standard care protocols."

## "Research" page content — Society's own research strategy (directly validates this hackathon's Track 2 framing)
"We don't fund research for research's sake. We fund it to find real treatments — and ultimately a cure."

**Stated strategic focus: "Strategic Focus on Treatable Pathways — We only fund research that has a clear route toward real-world treatments - including drug repurposing, cellular analysis, and genetic targeting. No vague science. Just focused progress."**

**"Fast-Tracked by Data Rarity"** — with <50 known patients globally, the Society coordinates research across the whole known population, capturing patient data as a key resource for research.

This is strong independent confirmation that drug repurposing is exactly the kind of contribution the disease's own research community considers highest-value — not just a hackathon-invented framing.

## George's Story (patient story, publicly shared by the family)
George was diagnosed with MVA at age 2, alongside rhabdomyosarcoma (rare liver cancer). Underwent a pioneering two-stage operation using Nano Knife Technology (first child in the UK to receive this treatment on the liver, coordinated with Professor Anil Dhawan). Today reported as "fit, healthy, and thriving." His parents founded the MVA Society after being told the condition was "untreatable" and to "wait and see." Note: this is a DIFFERENT MVA patient than the hackathon's case (not necessarily the same child) — treat as general disease-community context and precedent for cancer-surveillance/intervention approaches, not as data about the hackathon's specific proband, unless independently confirmed.
