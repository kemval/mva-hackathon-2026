# Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy Syndrome (Wikipedia overview)

Captured: 2026-08-31

## Definition
Mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndrome (MVA), also called Warburton-Anyane-Yeboa syndrome, is a rare autosomal recessive genetic disorder. Involves inappropriate chromosomal segregation in mitosis, resulting in some cells becoming aneuploid. Affects approximately 1 in 1,000,000 individuals.

## Genetic subtypes (six identified)
- **MVA1**: BUB1B (chromosome 15q15.1)
- **MVA2**: CEP57 (chromosome 11q21)
- **MVA3**: TRIP13 (chromosome 5p15)
- **MVA4**: CENATAC (chromosome 11q23)
- **MVA5**: SLF2 (chromosome 10q24)
- **MVA6**: SMC5 (chromosome 9q21)

Additional associated genes: BUB1, BUB3. All relate to spindle checkpoint function.

## Molecular mechanisms by gene
- BUB1/BUB1B/BUB3: spindle checkpoint control for chromosome splitting; mutations prevent proper segregation.
- CEP57: maintains spindle pole integrity; defects cause aberrant segregation.
- CENATAC: regulates the minor U12-dependent spliceosome, which controls cell-cycle regulation proteins; MVA involves deregulation of this process.
- SLF2/SMC5: essential for chromosomal segregation via centromeric/sister-chromatid cohesion.
- TRIP13: activates MAD2, engaging the spindle checkpoint; mutations impair checkpoint activation.

## Clinical presentation
**Very frequent**: peritoneal fluid excess (ascites), cataracts, Dandy-Walker malformation, epicanthus, glaucoma, micrognathia, increased nuchal translucency, short stature, ventriculomegaly.

**Frequent**: eye/vision abnormalities, global developmental delay, microcephaly, intellectual disability, triangular facial appearance.

**Occasional**: cardiovascular abnormalities, lung lobation defects, skeletal anomalies, genitalia abnormalities, cerebellar/corpus callosum agenesis, sleep apnea, cleft palate, clinodactyly, hypothyroidism, intrauterine growth restriction.

Increased tumor risk: acute lymphoblastic leukemia, nephroblastoma (Wilms tumor), rhabdomyosarcoma.

## Diagnosis
1. Karyotyping — detects chromosomal anomalies.
2. Genetic testing — identifies causal gene mutations.

Characteristic facial features noted: high forehead, midface hypoplasia, elongated philtrum, hypertelorism, epicanthal folds, short wide nose, depressed nasal bridge.

## Prognosis
Depends on the types of malformation presented in the individual; outcomes vary significantly.

## History
Initially described by Rudd et al. 1983; formal diagnosis name established by Warburton et al. 1991.
