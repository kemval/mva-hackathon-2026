# Clearance of p16Ink4a-positive senescent cells delays ageing-associated disorders

Authors: Baker DJ, Wijshake T, Tchkonia T, LeBrasseur NK, Childs BG, van de Sluis B, Kirkland JL, van Deursen JM
Journal: Nature, 2011 Nov 2;479(7372):232-6. doi: 10.1038/nature10600
Source: PMC3468323 (open access, NIH public access manuscript) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3468323/
Captured: 2026-08-31 (correction — previously logged as "paywalled, no PMC copy found"; a PMC copy does exist and is fetched here in full)

## Abstract

Advanced age is the main risk factor for most chronic diseases and functional deficits in humans, but the fundamental mechanisms that drive ageing remain largely unknown. Cellular senescence, which halts the proliferation of damaged or dysfunctional cells, is an important mechanism to constrain the malignant progression of tumour cells. Senescent cells accumulate in various tissues and organs with ageing and have been hypothesized to disrupt tissue structure and function because of the components they secrete. However, whether senescent cells are causally implicated in age-related dysfunction and whether their removal is beneficial has remained unknown. To address these fundamental questions, the authors made use of a biomarker for senescence, p16Ink4a, to design a novel transgene, INK-ATTAC, for inducible elimination of p16Ink4a-positive senescent cells upon administration of a drug. In the BubR1 progeroid mouse background, INK-ATTAC removes p16Ink4a-positive senescent cells upon drug treatment. In tissues — adipose tissue, skeletal muscle and eye — where p16Ink4a contributes to age-related pathologies, life-long removal of p16Ink4a-expressing cells delayed onset of these phenotypes. Late-life clearance attenuated progression of already-established age-related disorders. These data indicate cellular senescence is causally implicated in generating age-related phenotypes and that removal of senescent cells can prevent or delay tissue dysfunction and extend healthspan.

## Key methods and findings (full body, condensed)

- **Model**: INK-ATTAC transgene (adapted from the FAT-ATTAC apoptosis system) driven by the p16Ink4a promoter, enabling drug-inducible (AP20187) apoptosis specifically in p16Ink4a-positive senescent cells, bred onto a **BubR1 hypomorphic (BubR1^H/H)** genetic background.
- **BubR1 background biology (directly relevant to this project)**: "BubR1 encodes a key member of the mitotic checkpoint, a surveillance mechanism that ensures accurate chromosome segregation in mitosis by inhibiting the ubiquitin ligase activity of Cdc20-activated anaphase-promoting complex (APC^Cdc20) in the presence of unattached chromosomes." BubR1^H/H mice have markedly shortened lifespan and a range of age-related phenotypes: infertility, lordokyphosis, sarcopenia, cataracts, fat loss, cardiac arrhythmias, arterial wall stiffening, impaired wound healing, dermal thinning. "It has been proposed that BubR1 is a determinant of natural ageing, because levels of BubR1 decline markedly with age."
- **Result — life-long clearance**: AP20187-treated BubR1^H/H;INK-ATTAC mice had substantially delayed onset of lordokyphosis (sarcopenia marker) and cataracts, larger muscle fibre diameter, better treadmill exercise performance, and prevented loss of adipose tissue, versus untreated littermates.
- **Result — late-life clearance** (started at 5 months instead of weaning): still improved muscle fibre diameter, exercise performance, and fat depot size at 10 months — i.e., clearing senescent cells later in the disease course still helped, not just prevention from birth.
- **Important limitation found in the paper itself** (a real caveat for any Track 2 argument built on this): **cardiac arrhythmias and arterial wall stiffening — two other BubR1^H/H phenotypes — were NOT attenuated by senescent-cell clearance**, because these arise in a "p16Ink4a-independent fashion" (no INK-ATTAC induction was seen in heart/aorta). Cardiac failure is presumably the main cause of death in these mice, which is also given as the likely reason overall survival was not substantially extended by treatment despite the tissue-level improvements. **This means senolytic/senescence-clearing intervention in this model is not a cure-all — it improves specific p16Ink4a-driven tissue phenotypes (muscle, fat, eye) but not p16Ink4a-independent ones (cardiac).** Important to state explicitly in the Track 2 report rather than overclaiming broad benefit.
- **Mechanistic link to SASP**: the discussion explicitly ties benefit to reduced senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) — "acquisition of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), which enables cells to secrete a variety of growth factors, cytokines and proteases, contributes to age-related tissue dysfunction."
- **Safety signal**: "There were no overt side effects of senescent cell clearance in our model" — relevant, cautiously, to the safety-framing of a senolytic hypothesis, though this is a mouse transgenic-clearance system, not the human pharmacological senolytics (dasatinib+quercetin) that would actually be proposed — the paper itself does not test dasatinib/quercetin at all; that connection is drawn from later, separate literature (see `notes/04_drug_repurposing_methodology.md`).
