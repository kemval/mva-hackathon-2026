# Nephrocalcinosis in preterm infants — incidence, timing, and what "since birth" can mean

Captured 2026-09-02 for **Task 4** of `pipeline/gap_closure_brief.md`. Five sources, three
in full text via the **Europe PMC REST API** (the PMC HTML route is now behind a
reCAPTCHA — see the fetch log).

**Why this file exists.** The Track 1 report withdrew two acquired explanations for this
proband's nephrocalcinosis and now argues that congenital onset *raises* the prior for a
second monogenic cause. That reversal rests entirely on a four-word clause. This file
assembles what the paediatric literature actually says about preterm nephrocalcinosis, its
timing, and how the monogenic prior varies with gestational age.

---

## 1. Kim et al. 2023 — *BMC Pediatrics* 23:447. PMID 37684572, PMC10485931. **Full text.**

*"Etiology and prognosis of nephrocalcinosis according to gestational age in Korean children."*
464 children under 18 diagnosed with NC by ultrasonography, 2013–2022, Samsung Medical Centre.

**This is the source that bears directly on the adjudication.** Verbatim:

> "the monogenic mutation rate was significantly higher in full-term children (OR 10.02,
> 95% CI [2.464–40.786], p = 0.001)."

> "While the overall outcomes of pediatric NC are favorable, underlying monogenic causes
> should be studied, **especially in full-term patients without known clinical risk
> factors.**"

> "The mean age at first diagnosis of NC was 385.4 days."

> "In comparison of the preterm (mean GA 28 weeks and 2 days) and full-term (mean GA 38
> weeks and 2 days) groups, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, patent ductus arteriosus, and use of
> furosemide and vitamin D were more frequent in the preterm group."

> "the rate of spontaneous resolution was significantly higher in the preterm group (68.4%
> vs. 48.9%, p = 0.003)."

**LIMITS — what this paper does NOT show.** Single centre, retrospective, Korean population.
Genetic analysis was performed in only **56 of 464** patients, and who got tested was not
randomised — clinicians presumably tested the children who looked genetic, which is exactly
the confounding that would inflate an OR in this direction. It does not report age at first
diagnosis split by gestational age. It says nothing about MVA, BUB1B, or any chromosomal
instability syndrome. It cannot establish that any individual preterm child's NC is acquired.

## 2. Ruys / Gilbert et al. 2022 — *Pediatric Nephrology* 37:2121. PMID 35348900, PMC9587072. **Full text.**

*"Nephrocalcinosis in very low birth weight infants: incidence, associated factors, and
natural course."* 525 eligible infants <32+6 weeks and ≤1500 g; 265 included, case-control.

> "Cases had a diagnosis of NC at **kidney ultrasound performed at 35 weeks corrected GA**."

> "overall prevalence of NC was **17.1%** at 35 weeks corrected age."

> "Exposure to furosemide (OR [IC95%]: **1.26 [1.02; 1.57]**) and postnatal growth (1.65
> [1.04; 2.67]) were independent risk factors of NC."

> "**NC resolved 12–18 months after diagnosis in 61% of infants.**"

**LIMITS.** Single French tertiary unit, retrospective case-control. Neonates with congenital
malformations were **excluded**, so the cohort is by construction uninformative about
congenital-onset disease. Ultrasound was performed at one time point — 35 weeks corrected GA
— so the study cannot date onset, only detect prevalence at that moment. No genetic testing.

## 3. Narendra et al. 2001 — *Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed* 85:F207. PMID 11668166.
**Abstract only** — Europe PMC returned HTTP 404 for PMC1721318 (scanned-era deposit).

Prospective observational study, 101 preterm infants <32 weeks or <1500 g. The study design
is the quotable part:

> "Two renal ultrasound scans were performed, **the first at 1 month postnatal age** and the
> second at term or discharge."

> "Twenty three (23%) had abnormal ultrasound scans. **Sixteen (16%) had nephrocalcinosis.**"

> "The multifactorial origin, in particular, the association with extreme prematurity and
> severity of respiratory disease, is confirmed."

Multivariate predictors were "duration of ventilation, toxic gentamicin/vancomycin levels,
low fluid intake, and male sex" — note that **calcium and phosphate intake did not survive**,
and affected babies actually had *lower* calcium and phosphate intake.

**LIMITS.** Abstract only — recorded as a gap, not reasoned around. Single centre, n=101,
2001 practice. Nothing on genetics.

## 4. Bonnett / Kaplan et al. 1991 — *Arch Dis Child* 66:531. PMID 2025034. **Abstract only.**

79 infants <32 weeks, **serial** renal ultrasound. Incidence **26.6%**. Affected infants were
smaller (940 g) and less mature (26.9 weeks). "Infants who still required oxygen treatment at
28 days had a **62% chance** of developing renal calcification."

**LIMITS.** Abstract only; 1991 neonatal practice, which differs substantially from current.

## 5. Santos / Gupta et al. 2018 — *Matern Health Neonatol Perinatol* 4:23. PMID 30473868,
PMC6240934. **Full text.** — *the disconfirming source, sought deliberately*

Systematic review of 32 studies (20 on nephrocalcinosis/nephrolithiasis) of furosemide in
premature infants:

> "We found **no evidence that furosemide exposure increases the risk** of SNHL or NC/NL in
> premature infants, with varying quality of studies and found the **strength of evidence for
> both outcomes to be low.** The most common limitation in these studies was the lack of
> control for confounding factors."

**LIMITS.** A null with low-strength evidence is not a demonstration of no effect; the review
says so itself and calls for RCTs. It does not overturn source 2's adjusted OR — it bounds
how confidently furosemide can be named as *the* mechanism.

## 6. Kajii et al. 1998 — *Am J Med Genet* 78:245. PMID 9677059. **Abstract only.**
*Cross-check: does published MVA include nephrocalcinosis?*

Two unrelated infants with total premature chromatid separation and mosaic variegated
aneuploidy. Renal involvement is present but **is not nephrocalcinosis**:

> "Patient 1, a girl, in addition had a cleft palate, **multiple renal cysts, and Wilms tumor**
> of the left kidney."

**LIMITS.** Two patients, 1998, pre-BUB1B. **This is one case report, not the cohort search
that Task 2 specifies** — the absence of nephrocalcinosis here is weak evidence and must not
be reported as an exhaustive negative.

---

## Evidence tier

**Observational human**, four paediatric cohorts (n = 79 to 464) plus one systematic review.
No RCT. No data on MVA, BUB1B, or chromosomal instability syndromes anywhere in this set. All
inference to this proband is by population analogy.
